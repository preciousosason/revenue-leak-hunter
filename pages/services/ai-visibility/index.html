The opportunity is not to “trick AI.” It is to build a website that is genuinely useful, technically accessible, clearly structured, and easier to understand and reference.

01 / THE LEAK

You can have a strong brand and still be invisible at the moment of discovery.

Your website may explain what you sell perfectly to someone who already knows your name. But discovery starts earlier than that.

People ask questions. They compare approaches. They look for recommendations. Increasingly, some of those journeys happen inside AI-assisted search and answer experiences.

If your site does not clearly answer those questions, establish what your brand knows, and connect that expertise to useful pages, other sources can occupy that space instead.

That is the visibility leak.
02 / THE INVESTIGATION

I do not start with AI tricks.
I start with the foundation.

No one can guarantee that an AI system will cite or recommend a specific website. The useful work is improving the signals and assets that make your site easier to discover, understand, retrieve, and trust.

01

Technical Discovery

Crawlability, indexability, internal linking, page accessibility, canonical signals, and other foundations that determine whether important content can be found.

02

Question & Topic Coverage

The real questions people ask before buying, and whether your website contains useful pages that answer them with enough depth and clarity.

03

Answer-Ready Structure

Headings, definitions, comparisons, FAQs, supporting context, and page structures that make useful information easier to extract and understand.

04

Entity Clarity

How clearly the site communicates who the brand is, what it offers, which topics it has expertise in, and how its pages relate to one another.

05

Trust & Evidence

Authorship, first-hand expertise, references, proof, policies, business information, and other credibility signals that support the claims your content makes.

06

Measurement

A baseline for branded visibility, relevant prompts and queries, referral traffic, citations where observable, and changes worth monitoring over time.
