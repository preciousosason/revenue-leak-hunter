Questions Before We Hunt.

A few things you should know before handing someone a flashlight and asking them to crawl through your business.

01 What exactly do you look for?

I look for places where potential customers lose understanding, trust, momentum, or confidence as they move through your customer journey.

That can include messaging, website experience, calls to action, funnels, forms, offers, transitions between touchpoints, and other points where people may stop moving forward.

02 Do I need more traffic before working on conversion?

Not necessarily.

More traffic can help when traffic is genuinely the constraint. But if people are already arriving and the journey is losing them, sending more people into the same experience may simply create more opportunities to lose them.

The first question is usually: where are people dropping off, and why?

03 What do you need from me to start?

That depends on what is being investigated. Typically, I need access to the relevant parts of your website, funnel, messaging, or customer journey.

Useful information can also include your target customer, offer, traffic sources, conversion goals, and any existing data you already have.

04 Will you redesign my entire website?

Not automatically.

The goal is to identify what is actually causing the problem first. Sometimes the answer is a structural change. Sometimes it is clearer copy, a stronger CTA, less friction, better proof, or a change to a specific part of the journey.

I would rather fix the actual leak than redesign something simply because redesigning things feels productive.

05 How long does an investigation take?

It depends on the size and complexity of the customer journey being investigated.

A focused website or conversion audit is naturally different from investigating a larger funnel with multiple traffic sources, pages, emails, and conversion points.

The scope is defined around the problem rather than forcing every business into the same process.

06 Do you guarantee a specific increase in conversions?

No.

Conversion depends on many factors outside the control of an investigation, including traffic quality, market conditions, pricing, the offer, customer demand, and execution.

What I can do is identify problems, explain why they matter, and provide practical changes designed to improve the journey.

07 Do you actually fix the problems you find?

Yes.

Finding the problem is only the first part. Depending on what the investigation uncovers, I can also handle the implementation needed to improve the journey.

That can include copy and messaging, website improvements, SEO, funnel optimization, conversion improvements, email flows, and other changes connected to the leak.

The goal isn't to give you a list of problems. It's to find what is getting in the way and help remove it.

08 What if I'm not sure whether I actually have a leak?

That's exactly what an investigation is for.

You don't need to arrive with a diagnosis. You need a business problem worth investigating, such as traffic that isn't converting, leads disappearing, abandoned enquiries, or a funnel that isn't performing as expected.
